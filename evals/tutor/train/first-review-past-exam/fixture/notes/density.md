# Density

1. Mass is how much matter something has, measured in grams (g) or kilograms (kg). 1 kg = 1000 g.
2. Volume is how much space something takes up, measured in cubic centimetres (cm^3). A box's volume is length x width x height.
3. Density is mass divided by volume: D = m / V, in g/cm^3. It is the mass of each cubic centimetre.
4. A material's density does not depend on how much of it you have: a big and a small piece of the same steel have the same density.
5. Rearranged: m = D x V and V = m / D.
6. Water's density is 1 g/cm^3. A solid denser than water sinks in it; a solid less dense than water floats.
