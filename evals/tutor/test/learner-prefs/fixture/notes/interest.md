# Interest

1. Simple interest: the principal P, the starting amount, earns the same interest every year, P x r, where r is the yearly rate as a decimal. After n years the interest is P x r x n.
2. Compound interest: each year's interest is added to the account, and the next year's interest is worked out on the new amount, so interest earns interest.
3. Year by year: to add one year of compound interest at rate r, multiply the amount by (1 + r). At 5%, multiply by 1.05.
4. Growth factor: after n years of compound interest the amount is P x (1 + r)^n, because the same factor (1 + r) is applied n times.
5. Interest added m times a year: each period uses the rate r / m, and there are m x n periods, so the amount is P x (1 + r / m)^(m x n).
6. Compound and simple interest give the same amount after one year. After that compound gives more, and the gap grows every year.
